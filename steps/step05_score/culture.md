### Additional Editorial Category: Celebrity Trends, Viral AI Stories, and Curiosity

Do not restrict the newsletter to serious industry news, product launches, business developments, and practical updates.

The audience also wants to know about AI stories that are surprising, entertaining, controversial, culturally relevant, or simply fascinating.

Actively look for:

**1. Celebrity and public-figure AI stories**

* Celebrities using, endorsing, criticizing, or experimenting with AI.
* Viral AI-generated images, videos, voices, or impersonations involving public figures.
* AI-related controversies involving celebrities, artists, creators, or other well-known people.
* Major developments involving celebrity likeness, voice cloning, deepfakes, or digital replicas.

**2. Viral AI trends and internet culture**

* AI trends spreading across social media.
* AI-generated content attracting unusual public attention.
* New AI memes, challenges, creative formats, or unexpected uses.
* Stories showing how people are actually using AI in everyday life.

**3. Curiosity-driven AI stories**

* Unexpected things AI models can now do.
* Strange, surprising, funny, or counterintuitive AI behavior.
* Unusual experiments, demonstrations, and creative applications.
* Impressive AI creations or discoveries that make people want to see the result.
* Unexpected failures, bizarre outputs, or surprising real-world consequences.

**4. Human-interest and controversy stories**

* Stories about how AI is changing people's lives, jobs, relationships, creativity, or entertainment.
* Public reactions to AI developments.
* Significant debates involving artists, creators, celebrities, or communities.
* Unusual incidents that reveal something meaningful about AI's capabilities or limitations.

### How to evaluate these stories

Judge these stories using their own editorial value, not only their technical or financial importance.

Consider:

* **Curiosity:** Would someone genuinely want to hear what happened?
* **Surprise:** Is the story unexpected or remarkable?
* **Visual or demonstration value:** Would seeing the result make the story more compelling?
* **Cultural relevance:** Is it part of a meaningful or rapidly spreading trend?
* **Conversation value:** Would people want to discuss or share it?
* **AI relevance:** Does AI play a central role in the story rather than being a superficial connection?
* **Credibility:** Is the story verified, and is the context represented accurately?

Do not select a story merely because a celebrity is mentioned or a headline sounds bizarre. The AI connection must be meaningful, and viral claims must be checked.

### Protect variety in the final episode

A strong episode should include a mix of:

* Major AI developments people need to know.
* Practical updates people can use immediately.
* Important industry and business developments.
* Celebrity, viral, entertaining, and curiosity-driven stories.

Do not allow serious news to eliminate every entertaining story. Equally, do not let viral stories crowd out important launches, policy changes, or useful opportunities.

Rank stories within their editorial context, then choose a balanced final lineup based on overall importance, audience usefulness, curiosity, and available runtime.

If a celebrity or curiosity story is genuinely one of the biggest AI stories of the day, it can rank near the top. Otherwise, use it to add variety and interest later in the episode.
