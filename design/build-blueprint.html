Two ratings, both out of 10. The accuracy rating answers one question: how faithfully does this model write from the text it is given, ignoring price? The overall rating is for this job as a whole: accuracy first, then writing quality, price, and whether the model is current and easy to get.

Where the accuracy rating comes from. Where the Evidence column says Measured, it is the leaderboard figure put on a scale: 3.5% or lower is 9, up to 4.5% is 8.5, up to 5.5% is 8, up to 6.5% is 7.5, up to 8% is 7, up to 9.5% is 6.5, up to 11% is 6, and so on down. Two models lose points for often declining to answer. Where it says Judged, there is no measurement, and the figure is my estimate from the model's class; treat it as a guess. I am a Claude model rating Claude models among others; they follow the same figures as the rest.

A surprise worth knowing. On this measure, bigger is not more accurate. Several large, expensive models measured worse than small cheap ones, which is why the judged ratings for unmeasured large models stay at 7.5 to 8 and no higher.

Read the last column with care. The leaderboard tests short summaries of one document, judged by another model. Our task is harder: several sources per story, a comparison of how they differ, and a spoken style. It also has no figures for the newest models, and where it says “earlier version” the number belongs to an older release of that model. It tells us which models to test, not which one will win.

What free costs. Free tiers have daily and per-minute limits, no guarantee of availability, and Google's free tier uses prompts to improve its products outside Europe. Our prompts are public news articles, so that matters little here. Running a model on your own machine without a graphics card is slow: I'd expect an hour or more per day's run, which is untested.

Proposed approach. Make the model a setting, so any of these can be swapped in. Run the same ten real stories through all six models rated 8 or higher, with Claude Haiku 4.5 as the comparison, and pick by how many sentences fail the check and how they read. Then use a second model from a different maker for the checking pass, since two different models rarely make the same mistake.
