### Writing Style and Flow

- **Start with the news itself.** No introductory setup, generic opening, or phrases like "Today, we're looking at."
- **Use honest open loops.** When a later segment genuinely builds on an earlier one, create a brief connection, such as "Hold on to that number; it matters again in the safety segment." The later segment must actually deliver on that connection. Never manufacture suspense or promise a payoff that doesn't exist.
- **Vary sentence length naturally.** Follow a longer sentence with a shorter one when it improves rhythm and clarity. Every sentence must still be complete. Avoid artificial fragments added just for dramatic effect.
- **Keep transitions purposeful.** Connect stories when there is a meaningful relationship between them. Otherwise, move naturally to the next story without forcing a connection.
- **Prioritize clarity over drama.** The script should sound natural when spoken aloud, not like clickbait, an advertisement, or a theatrical performance.

### Banned Phrases and Clichés

Never use these expressions:
- "Let's dive in"
- "It's important to remember"
- "Think about it"
- "In a world where"
- "Buckle up"
- "Game-changer"
- "Landscape" as a generic metaphor for an industry or field
- "Delve"

Avoid replacing these with equivalent clichés. Express the actual idea directly.

### Editorial Integrity

- Every open loop must have a real, verifiable payoff later in the script.
- Never distort a story's significance to create tension or improve engagement.
- Don't add transitions merely to make separate stories sound connected.
- Natural pacing should come from sentence structure and the material itself, not forced drama.
