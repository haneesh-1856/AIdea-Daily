# Weekend Edition sample: the week of 2026-10-27 to 2026-11-01

## Script

This is a look back at the week in AI, with the five stories that mattered most and what has been reported about each since they first ran.

**Anthropic files for its IPO**

We start with Anthropic, which filed an S-1 with the SEC for a proposed initial public offering, as first reported on Tuesday. The company plans to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT. The number of shares and the price range have not been determined, and the shares cannot be sold until the registration statement becomes effective. TechCrunch reported revenue of $16.3 billion for the first half of 2026, up from $1.2 billion a year earlier. Wired noted that the filing leaves out some figures, including third-quarter financials and compute contract totals.

The coverage kept coming. On Wednesday, The Decoder reported that Anthropic posted its first GAAP operating profit in the second quarter, at $974 million, alongside long-term compute commitments of $780 to $810 billion. It also reported that net revenue retention sits at roughly 900 percent. Also on Wednesday, TechCrunch reported that Amazon is likely the single largest financial beneficiary of the IPO, with convertible notes of $6.2 billion in principal outstanding as of June 30. Amazon's own filings valued its note holdings at $97.9 billion. The Verge looked at Anthropic's governance structure, quoting University of Pennsylvania law professor Elizabeth Pollman calling it a structure that has not been used before. Ars Technica focused on the risk factors, including those that could pose, in Anthropic's words, catastrophic or existential risks to humanity. Semafor reported that the company expects its energy costs to rise. Semafor's Liz offered an ink-blot reading: believers see a fast-growing company, while skeptics see a loss-making one with $81 billion in net losses. The Decoder also reported that Anthropic used its IPO letter to renew its pledge that Claude will never show ads.

On Thursday, Semafor said the key number is an 85% gross profit margin on the core work of running existing models, which it said excludes the cost of training new models. TechCrunch reported that Anthropic launched reporting tools to show companies what their AI spending is buying. On Friday, The Verge spoke with SemiAnalysis analyst Joey Brookhart, who said there was not much to nitpick in the filing, and The Verge also ran a piece arguing that Anthropic's plan is to build God or die trying. On Saturday, The Guardian published a video explainer on the 92-page risk factor document. And on Sunday, a Hacker News piece argued that the filing helped crush tech stocks, saying Google stock fell by 12% on Thursday and the tech sector is down 10% from its high.

**Gemini 4 Carbon**

On Thursday, Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon, two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon. Google calls it its strongest model, with state-of-the-art cybersecurity capabilities. It ranks first on version 5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 59. Access is limited for now, with a subset of cyber defenders getting it first. Ars Technica was the most skeptical, saying it is a model users can't use yet.

Since then, on Friday, Hacker News carried the Artificial Analysis post calling Carbon the new number 1 on the Intelligence Index v5. It said Carbon scores 59 on that index, and that it is second only to Claude Opus 5.5 on the Coding Agent Index, 59 against 62. It also reported that Google is offering a 50% launch discount with no confirmed end date, and that hallucination rate rose from 4% to 11%, still the second lowest of any model.

**Mistral's Le Chonk**

On Friday, Mistral released the weights of Mistral Large 4, nicknamed Le Chonk, pitching it for coding and defensive cybersecurity. It is a Mixture-of-Experts model with a 1M token context window, and the weights are on Hugging Face. Mistral says it scores 48% on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index and rose 13 percentage points to 41% on Terminal-Bench 4.0. Vals AI ranked it 17th of 30 on the Vals Index at 56.42%.

On Saturday, The Decoder reported the first independent test. It said the model does well on coding but scores 26 points on Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index, behind open Chinese models like DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3. It also reported a speed of 161 tokens per second and a 96.6 percent refusal rate for harmful cyber requests. And on Claude Opus 5.5, it said Large 4 won only 22 percent of blind code matchups.

**GPT-6.1 Terra**

On Tuesday, OpenAI introduced GPT-6.1 Terra, a fast model for computer use, which became available in GitHub Copilot. Pricing is $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens. Vals AI ranked it 10th of 28 on the Vals Index at 60.17%, though it was weaker on legal and tax benchmarks.

On Wednesday, The Decoder reported that OpenAI's Ted Sanders shared a rule of thumb for choosing between models. In his words, if you care about quality only, pick Astra; if you're a bit price sensitive, pick Sol; and if you're very price sensitive, pick Terra or Luna.

**Claude Code extension backdoored**

On Saturday, reports said a malicious version of the Claude Code extension, version 2.1.313, was available in Cursor's extension marketplace. Cursor said it affected at most 5,000 users. It tried to install a persistent SSH backdoor, and Cursor took the whole marketplace offline before restoring it. Neither Cursor nor Anthropic has explained how attackers got control of the account it was distributed through. Nothing new was reported after that. It mattered this week because, as Ars Technica framed it, this was a supply-chain compromise of an official distribution channel.

That is the week in AI, and we will be back with the next briefing.
